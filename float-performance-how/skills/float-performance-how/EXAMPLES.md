## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's a Problem | How to Fix |
|---------|-------------------|------------|
| **Generic strength statements** | "I'm a strong communicator" is vague and unmemorable | Be specific: "I synthesize complex tradeoffs into clear narratives that align stakeholders" |
| **Listing values without behaviors** | Claiming "I demonstrated ownership" without showing HOW | Provide specific behaviors: "I took ownership by proactively coordinating with CS..." |
| **Claiming team achievements as solo** | "I delivered X" when it was a team effort damages credibility | Clarify: "I led the technical approach while Sarah handled stakeholder alignment" |
| **Missing level calibration** | Not connecting work to level expectations leaves reviewers guessing | Reference leveling framework language: "IC4-level execution through..." |
| **Passive voice obscuring contribution** | "The project was delivered" hides your specific actions | Use active voice: "I scoped the 3-phase approach and aligned 4 stakeholders" |
| **No differentiation from peers** | Strengths that anyone at your level has aren't differentiating | Identify what makes YOU uniquely effective: your specific approach or combination of strengths |
| **Values name-dropping** | Mentioning values without connecting to impact | Show cause-effect: "Living 'Customers First' by prioritizing zero-disruption migration led to..." |
| **Skipping refinement** | First-draft examples lack polish and miss opportunities | Iterate through Step 6 refinement checklist; strengthen weak areas |

## Success Criteria

You're done with the HOW when you have:

- ✓ 2-3 key strengths clearly identified and evidenced across multiple outcomes
- ✓ Your work explicitly connected to Float's values with behavioral examples
- ✓ Clear calibration showing you're operating at or above your level
- ✓ 1-2 compelling narrative examples that integrate:
  - Context and challenge
  - Your specific actions and decisions
