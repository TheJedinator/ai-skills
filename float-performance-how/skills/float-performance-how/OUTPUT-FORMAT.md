## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

**Don't:**
- Name strengths without showing HOW they manifested
- List values without connecting to specific behaviors
- Claim team achievements as solely your own
- Use generic "I'm a strong X" statements
- Ignore level calibration or assume you're above level without evidence
- Write in passive voice that obscures your contribution
- Focus only on technical details without business context
- Settle for first draft - examples need refinement
- Over-claim or under-claim your impact

**Do:**
- Show strengths through specific actions and decisions
- Demonstrate values through concrete behaviors
- Clarify your unique contribution within collaborative work
- Articulate specific, differentiated strengths
- Calibrate honestly using level framework language
- Use active voice showing your agency
- Connect technical work to business outcomes
- Iterate and refine your examples
- Be honest and authentic about your impact

### Example 1: API Performance Optimization

**Context & Challenge:**
Our core transaction API was the #1 source of customer complaints (30 tickets/week) and was blocking 3 strategic roadmap features due to unreliable performance during peak hours. Previous attempts to fix it had failed because they addressed symptoms rather than root causes, and there was no clear owner driving a comprehensive solution.

**Your Actions:**
I took ownership of diagnosing and solving this end-to-end. I led a week-long investigation that identified 3 root causes (N+1 queries, missing indexes, poor caching strategy), then independently scoped a 3-phase approach that balanced quick wins with sustainable fixes. I synthesized technical constraints and business impact into a clear proposal that aligned Engineering, Product, and GTM on priorities, eliminating 2 weeks of previous decision thrash. I implemented the solution across 12 high-frequency endpoints, built a performance monitoring dashboard with automated alerting, and coordinated with the CS team to improve error messages and documentation—going beyond my initial scope because it was critical to the customer experience. When I discovered that our caching approach wouldn't scale to our growth projections, I pivoted the design mid-stream and rallied stakeholder support for the better long-term approach.

**Outcomes & Impact:**
- **Customer Satisfaction:** Reduced "slow dashboard" support tickets by 60% (30/week → 12/week), improved customer NPS for dashboard experience from 7.2 to 8.5 (+18%), eliminated timeout errors for 95% of customers
- **Product Innovation:** Unblocked 3 strategic roadmap features that required reliable API performance
- **Operational Excellence:** Reduced API p95 latency by 75% (800ms → 200ms), decreased database load by 40%, reduced on-call pages by 80%

**Key Strengths Demonstrated:**
- **Execution through ambiguity**: Independently scoped a complex problem that had stumped others, broke it into clear phases, identified dependencies across 3 teams, and pivoted technical approach mid-stream when data showed the original plan wouldn't scale—all while delivering ahead of original timeline
- **Strategic communication & alignment**: Synthesized complex technical tradeoffs into a clear narrative that aligned Engineering, Product, and GTM stakeholders, eliminating weeks of decision thrash and enabling decisive action on a high-stakes problem
- **Systems-level ownership**: Took accountability beyond my role (API performance) to improve the entire customer experience—proactively engaged CS to understand pain points, improved error messages and docs, created runbooks for on-call, and built monitoring to prevent regression

**Values Connection:**
- **Put Customers First**: Made customer impact the north star—when I discovered our "quick fix" would cause customer-facing issues during migration, I advocated strongly for the 2-week-longer approach that ensured zero customer disruption, even though it delayed my other work
- **Be An Owner**: Didn't stop at fixing the API performance (my assigned scope)—I owned the entire customer experience by improving error messages, documentation, and CS runbooks, and created monitoring systems to prevent future issues
- **Raise The Bar**: Refused to accept the "quick fix" that previous attempts had tried—insisted on solving root causes to set a higher standard for API reliability, and created reusable patterns and knowledge that elevated the entire team's approach to performance work

**Level Calibration:**
This demonstrates IC4-level execution through independently scoping and driving a high-impact, cross-functional initiative with minimal direction. It shows above-level indicators in scope (influenced org-level priorities by unblocking strategic roadmap items) and technical leadership (set patterns and direction that other teams adopted). The systems thinking and proactive stakeholder management align with IC5-level expectations for owning outcomes across boundaries and creating scalable solutions.
